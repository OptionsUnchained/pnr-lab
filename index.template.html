How the estimate works

PNR is the nearest modeled move from the evaluation price where account NLV reaches zero. EPR is a separate price stress input. A 42% PNR with a 25% EPR does not mean only 17% until zero equity. The displayed EPR boundary estimate is not a margin-call cushion. It assumes a fixed zero-equity price and fixed EPR; volatility, time and other holdings can change that boundary.

The model uses European Black–Scholes with continuous dividend yield, inferred IV per leg (or your IV override), and calendar days / 365. For every candidate price: equity = current account NLV + cash adjustment − costs + change in signed modeled position value. Future time decay is included once; entry premiums do not get added again to NLV.

With an IV override, P/L is anchored to the model’s current price; a mismatch with your midpoint is flagged. No American early exercise, discrete dividends, automatic skew dynamics, broker concentration charges, cross-underlying offsets or liquidation policy are modeled. Expired options use intrinsic settlement and are flagged; physical exercise/assignment and subsequent share positions must be entered manually. A 100-share multiplier is assumed for standard equity options.

Root search covers declines approaching 100% and rallies up to 1,000%, returning the first sampled crossing. “No crossing found” is a search limit, not a guarantee of safety. Complex nonmonotonic hedged portfolios may require finer analysis. EPR stress losses are not an actual buying-power requirement.
